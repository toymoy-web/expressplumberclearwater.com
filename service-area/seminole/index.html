City Overview

Plumbing In Seminole, Florida

Seminole is a city of about 19,400 residents in west-central Pinellas County, between Clearwater and St. Petersburg. It was incorporated in 1970, which makes it the youngest city in the county, even though farmers settled the area after the Civil War. The city is surrounded by unincorporated Pinellas County enclaves in every direction, so one street can be inside the city limits while the next is not. Our emergency dispatch covers all of it around the clock.

Housing & Pipes

Homes Built Between The 1940s And 1970s

Most single-family homes in Seminole date from the 1940s through the 1970s, with newer pockets such as Seminole Grove Estates built in the late 1970s and early 1980s. Houses from that range often still have original galvanized supply lines, aging drain lines, and water heaters on their second or third replacement. We check what is actually in the walls before suggesting any bigger repair. A short pressure test or camera look usually tells us more than the age of the house does.

Local History

A Ridge, A Railroad, And Citrus Groves

Early settlers were drawn to the Seminole area for its drainage and higher ground along the ridge. The Tampa and Gulf Coast Railway opened in late 1914, and citrus groves spread across the area soon after. Residential growth took off after the Second World War, and the old rail route is now the Pinellas Trail, which runs through the middle of town. Many of the older streets we serve sit just off that corridor.

Neighborhoods & Landmarks

Areas We Cover In Seminole

We regularly work around Seminole Boulevard, Lake Seminole Park, Seminole City Park, and Orange Blossom Park along the Pinellas Trail. Boca Ciega Millennium Park, Walsingham Park, and the Seminole City Center shopping district, built where the old Seminole Mall stood, all fall inside our normal dispatch routes. Subdivisions such as Seminole Grove Estates, and the unincorporated communities that still use Seminole mailing addresses, are part of the same service zone.

Before We Arrive

If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak In Seminole

Shut Off The Main Valve

Find the main shutoff where the line enters the house or at the meter near the street, and close it before water spreads.

Kill Power To Wet Areas

Turn off the breaker for any outlet or appliance near standing water, and never touch a wet switch.

Clear The Immediate Area

Move rugs, furniture, and boxes away from the leak so they stay dry while help is on the way.

FAQs

Questions From Seminole

Is Seminole the same as Seminole County?

No. We serve the city of Seminole in Pinellas County, on the Gulf Coast between Clearwater and St. Petersburg. Seminole County is a separate county near Orlando, and we do not dispatch there.

Who supplies drinking water in Seminole?

It depends on the address, since the city is surrounded by unincorporated areas and more than one provider serves the area. Your water bill names your utility, and we ask because it helps us anticipate wear on fittings and whether a water test makes sense.

Do you work on the older homes from the 1940s through the 1970s?

Yes. These homes often need different fittings and a careful look at galvanized supply lines or aging drains before we recommend any larger repair.

Do you cover homes with Largo mailing addresses near Seminole?

Yes. Several communities around Seminole are unincorporated and use Largo or Seminole mailing addresses. If your home is in the Seminole area, we dispatch to it.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Seminole?

Yes, including the 33772, 33776, and 33777 ZIP codes, from the Seminole Boulevard corridor to the neighborhoods around Lake Seminole Park.

Is emergency service available in Seminole?

Yes, our dispatch line is answered 24 hours a day, every day, for burst pipes, active leaks, and no-hot-water calls anywhere in Seminole.
