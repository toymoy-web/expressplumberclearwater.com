City Overview

Plumbing In Tarpon Springs, Florida

Tarpon Springs is a city of about 25,100 residents on the Anclote River at the northern end of Pinellas County. It was settled in the 1870s and incorporated in 1887, and it grew first as a winter resort. Nearly half of the city's total area is water, which shapes almost everything about its drainage and plumbing. Our emergency dispatch covers all of Tarpon Springs around the clock.

Housing & Pipes

Greektown, Spring Bayou, And Older Buildings

Downtown, Spring Bayou, and the Greektown Historic District hold some of the oldest buildings in northern Pinellas County, from vernacular homes to former sponge-packing warehouses. Greektown, bounded by the Anclote River, Tarpon Avenue, and Spring Bayou, was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 2014. Work on buildings like these starts with finding out what is actually there, because original pipe, later patches, and newer additions often share one wall.

Local History

Sponge Divers On The Anclote

Greek immigrants began arriving in the 1890s, and in 1905 John Cocoris recruited divers from Greece to bring sponge diving to Tarpon Springs. The Sponge Docks and Dodecanese Boulevard remain the city's best-known landmark, and St. Nicholas Greek Orthodox Cathedral anchors Greektown. The city has long had one of the highest shares of Greek-American residents of any city in the country.

Before We Arrive

If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak In Tarpon Springs

Shut Off The Main Valve

Find the main shutoff at the meter near the street or where the line enters the building, and close it before water spreads.

Kill Power To Wet Areas

Turn off the breaker for any outlet or appliance near standing water, and never touch a wet switch.

Clear The Immediate Area

Move rugs, furniture, and boxes away from the leak so they stay dry while help is on the way.

Do you work on the older buildings in Greektown and downtown?

Yes. Older buildings often have mixed original and replacement pipe, so we find out what is in the wall before recommending a repair.

Can storm surge flooding damage my plumbing?

Yes. Flood water can damage water heaters, shutoffs, pumps, and low drain lines, and it can force sewage back through drains. After a flood we check the plumbing before the water is turned back on.
