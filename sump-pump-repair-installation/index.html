Sump Pump Repair

Rare In Florida, Critical Where It Exists

Most Clearwater homes are built on a slab with no basement, so a sump pump is not standard equipment here. Where one is installed, it usually protects something specific, such as a below-grade garage, a crawl space, a drainage pit, or a low-lying yard. When that pump fails during a summer downpour, there is rarely a second line of defense.

Why This Happens Here

A High Water Table And Heavy Summer Rain

Pinellas County sits on a narrow peninsula with a high water table, and Florida's summer afternoon storms can drop a lot of rain in a short time. Groundwater rises, low spots fill, and a pump that sits idle most of the year is suddenly asked to run for hours. That is when worn float switches, stuck check valves, and clogged intakes show up.

Know The Limits

What A Sump Pump Can And Cannot Do

A sump pump removes groundwater, seepage, and runoff that collect in a pit. It does not stop storm surge. During Hurricane Helene in September 2024, surge from the Gulf and Tampa Bay flooded neighborhoods across Pinellas County, with a peak of about 6.5 feet at Clearwater Beach, and no residential pump keeps pace with that. For surge, the answer is elevation, flood protection, and fast water damage restoration.

Common Signs

Signs Your Sump Pump Needs Attention

Runs Constantly

A pump that never shuts off may have a stuck float, a failed check valve, or a pit that fills faster than the pump can empty it.

Never Runs At All

A pump that stays silent during heavy rain may have lost power, a failed switch, or a seized motor.

Grinding Or Rattling

Loud, rough running often means worn bearings, debris in the impeller, or a pump that is on its way out.

Standing Water In The Pit

Water that rises and does not drain, or a musty smell around the pit, points to a clog, a bad check valve, or a failed pump.

Before You Call

A Quick Check You Can Do Safely

If you can reach the pit without standing in water, check that the pump is plugged into a working outlet and that the float can move freely. Never touch a pump or its cord while you are standing in water. If a breaker trips or you see water near an outlet, stay out, shut off the power at the panel, and call us.

Repair vs. Replace

Fix It Or Replace It

Repair

  • Fits a failed float switch or check valve
  • Lower cost on a newer pump
  • Fast turnaround
  • Not a fix for a worn-out motor

Replace

  • Right call when the motor is worn or the housing is corroded
  • A chance to size the pump to the pit
  • Battery backup can be added
  • Higher upfront cost
Pump Types

Submersible Or Pedestal

A submersible pump sits inside the pit and runs quietly, while a pedestal pump keeps its motor above the pit and is easier to reach for service. The right choice depends on pit depth, how often the pump runs, and how much water it has to move. We recommend based on your pit, not on what is easiest to sell.

FAQs

Common Questions

Do Florida homes really need a sump pump?

Most do not. Slab-on-grade homes with no basement usually rely on grading and drainage instead. A sump pump makes sense for a below-grade space, a crawl space, or a low spot that collects water.

Can a sump pump protect me from storm surge?

No. A sump pump handles groundwater and runoff, not surge flooding from the Gulf or Tampa Bay. After a surge event, fast drying is what limits the damage.

What is the difference between a sump pump and a sewage ejector pump?

A sump pump moves groundwater and runoff, while a sewage ejector pump moves wastewater from fixtures below the sewer line. They are different equipment and we can inspect either one.

How often should I test my pump?

Before storm season and after any long dry spell, pour a bucket of water into the pit and confirm the pump starts, empties it, and shuts off.

Do I need a battery backup?

If the pump protects something you cannot afford to flood, a battery backup keeps it running through the power outages that often come with storms.
