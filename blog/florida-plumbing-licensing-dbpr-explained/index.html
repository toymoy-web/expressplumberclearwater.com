Understanding Florida's Plumbing Licensing System (DBPR Explained)

Not every state licenses plumbers the same way. Here's how Florida actually does it, and what to check before hiring one.

Written by the Clearwater Express Plumbing Team · September 16, 2026

Intro

Why This Actually Matters

Most homeowners never think about how plumbing licensing works until they're comparing quotes from different companies. Understanding the real system makes it easier to know what you're actually paying for, and what to verify before letting someone work on your home.

The Agency

Florida's Department Of Business & Professional Regulation

In Florida, plumbing contractors are licensed at the state level through the Department of Business and Professional Regulation, commonly abbreviated DBPR. This is different from states that license only at the county or city level, and it means a properly licensed Florida plumber has met a consistent statewide standard, not a patchwork of local requirements.

License Types

Certified vs. Registered Contractors

Florida distinguishes between certified contractors, who can work statewide, and registered contractors, who are licensed to work only within specific local jurisdictions that recognize their local competency exam. Both are legitimate license types, but the practical coverage area differs.

What Licensing Actually Verifies

Not Just A Piece Of Paper

A Florida plumbing license requires passing a trade competency exam and carrying required insurance. It doesn't guarantee a specific company's customer service or pricing, but it does verify a baseline of tested trade knowledge and legal accountability that unlicensed work simply doesn't have.

The Building Code Connection

How Licensing Ties To Inspections

Licensed plumbing work is expected to meet the Florida Building Code's plumbing provisions, the same standards local building departments inspect against for permitted work. That connection between licensing and code compliance is part of why unlicensed work can create real problems at resale or insurance claim time, even if it seemed to function fine for years.

Checking A License

How To Verify Before Hiring

Florida's DBPR maintains a public license verification system where homeowners can check a contractor's license status directly. It takes a few minutes and is a reasonable step before any significant plumbing work, especially anything requiring a permit.

Permits

When A Permit Is Actually Required

Not every plumbing job requires a permit, minor repairs like fixing a leaking faucet generally don't, but water heater replacements, repiping, and sewer line work typically do in most Pinellas County jurisdictions. A licensed contractor pulling the correct permit protects you if an issue arises later, especially at resale.

Out-Of-State Companies

Why This Trips Up Some Homeowners

A national chain operating in Florida still needs its technicians properly licensed under Florida's specific system, not just a license from wherever the company is headquartered. It's worth asking directly if you're working with a large multi-state company, since licensing requirements genuinely vary state to state.

Insurance & Bonding

The Other Half Of Legitimate Licensing

Beyond the trade exam itself, Florida licensing requires contractors to carry liability insurance and, in many cases, a surety bond. Those requirements exist specifically to protect homeowners if something goes wrong on a job, which is part of why verifying a license matters beyond just confirming trade competency.

Continuing Education

Licensing Isn't A One-Time Event

Florida requires licensed contractors to complete continuing education to renew their license periodically, which means the system is built around staying current rather than a credential earned once and never revisited. That ongoing requirement is part of what separates a maintained, active license from one that's technically valid but stale.

A Quick Recap

The Short Version If You're Skimming

Florida licenses plumbers at the state level through DBPR, requiring a trade exam plus insurance and often bonding, with the license tied to real, checkable public records. Verifying it before hiring takes a few minutes and is worth doing for anything beyond a trivial repair.

A Note On Trust

Why We Bring This Up Unprompted

Most homeowners never ask a plumber directly about licensing, and most companies never bring it up unless asked. We think that's backwards for a trade where the license genuinely matters, which is part of why we're comfortable explaining our own licensing plainly rather than waiting to be asked.

A Broader Point

Licensing As One Signal Among Several

A license is a real, verifiable baseline, but it's not the only thing worth considering when choosing a plumber. How clearly a company explains pricing, how it handles a mistake, and how it communicates during a job all matter too, and licensing is simply the one you can actually check in advance.

FAQs

Common Questions

Is unlicensed plumbing work ever legal in Florida?

Very minor work you do yourself on your own home is generally fine, but hiring an unlicensed person to perform contracted plumbing work is not, and it removes your legal protection if something goes wrong.

Do all your technicians carry the same license type?

Our technicians are licensed appropriately for the work performed in our service area, consistent with Florida DBPR requirements.

What happens if I hire someone unlicensed and something goes wrong?

You generally lose the legal protections and recourse that come with licensed, permitted work, and insurance claims tied to unlicensed work can also be more difficult.

Does a license also mean the contractor carries insurance?

Florida licensing requires liability insurance and, in many cases, bonding, both of which exist specifically to protect homeowners if something goes wrong.

Can I ask a company to show me their license directly?

Yes, and a legitimate company should have no hesitation providing that information or pointing you to the public verification system.

Does a license expire, and how would I know if it lapsed?

Yes, licenses require periodic renewal, and the public verification system shows current status, including whether a license has lapsed.

Is there a difference between a licensed plumber and a handyman doing plumbing work?

Yes, a handyman without a plumbing license shouldn't be performing work that legally requires one, regardless of how capable they seem.

Does licensing differ for residential versus commercial plumbing work?

The underlying license type can differ in scope, so it's worth confirming a contractor's license actually covers the kind of job you need done.

Can I do my own plumbing permit work if I own the home?

Florida allows owner-builder permits in some circumstances, but the rules are specific and don't apply to work performed by a hired, unlicensed third party.

Are DBPR license records searchable by company name as well as individual name?

Yes, the public verification system allows searching by either, which is useful when checking a company rather than a specific technician.

Is a license the same thing as a business registration?

No, they're separate, a business can be registered to operate while the trade license itself covers the actual plumbing competency and legal standing.
