Hydro Jetting vs. Snaking: Which Drain Cleaning Method Do You Need?

Both work. They're just built for different problems.

Written by the Clearwater Express Plumbing Team · September 16, 2026

DISPATCH LINE
(727) 931-8107
Call Now
Intro

Two Real Tools, Two Different Jobs

Cable snaking and hydro jetting are both legitimate drain clearing methods, but they're built to solve different problems, and using the wrong one is a common reason a clog comes back a few weeks after it seemed fixed.

How Snaking Works

A Mechanical Approach

A cable snake uses a rotating metal cable with a cutting or grabbing head to physically break through or pull out a blockage. It's fast, effective for a single, well-defined clog, and generally the lower-cost option for a straightforward job.

How Hydro Jetting Works

A Full-Pipe Cleaning Approach

Hydro jetting uses a high-pressure water stream, often 3,000 to 4,000 PSI, to scour the entire interior wall of the pipe, not just punch through the immediate blockage. It removes grease buildup, mineral scale, and root intrusion along the full length treated, rather than just clearing a path through it.

When Snaking Is Actually The Right Call

Simple, Isolated Clogs

A single clog with no history of recurrence, especially in a fixture-level drain like a bathroom sink, is often well-suited to snaking. It's faster and less expensive when the problem really is that contained.

When Hydro Jetting Is Actually The Right Call

Recurring Clogs Or Root Intrusion

If the same drain has clogged more than once in a relatively short period, or a camera inspection shows grease buildup or root intrusion along the pipe wall, hydro jetting addresses the underlying buildup rather than just punching through it again. It's the more thorough, longer-lasting fix for these situations.

Can They Cause Damage

A Common, Reasonable Concern

Both methods are safe for standard residential pipe when performed correctly. The real risk is using hydro jetting on already severely compromised or very old, fragile pipe without first confirming its condition via camera, which is why a professional inspection before jetting matters for older lines specifically.

Cost Differences

Why One Typically Costs More

Hydro jetting generally costs more than snaking because it takes longer, requires specialized equipment, and treats the entire pipe length rather than just clearing a path through one blockage. That higher upfront cost is often offset by not needing to repeat the service again in a few months for the same recurring clog.

Combining Both Methods

Sometimes The Right Answer Is Both

In some cases, an initial snake clears an urgent blockage immediately, and hydro jetting is scheduled afterward as a more thorough follow-up once the immediate emergency is resolved. This staged approach makes sense when a drain needs to be usable again right away but also has a documented history of buildup worth addressing properly.

Kitchen Vs. Main Line Clogs

Why Location Changes The Calculus

A kitchen sink clog close to the fixture is usually a quick, low-cost snake job regardless of buildup history, since the affected section is short and easy to reach. A main line clog affecting multiple fixtures throughout the house is a different scale of problem, and that's where hydro jetting's ability to clean the entire line length matters more.

How Often Jetting Is Needed

Not An Every-Visit Requirement

Hydro jetting isn't something a healthy drain needs repeated regularly. Once a line has been thoroughly cleaned, most homes go years before needing it again, assuming normal household use and no new root intrusion. It's a periodic deep clean, not a routine maintenance item like changing an air filter.

What A Camera Inspection Adds

Seeing Before Deciding

A camera inspection before either method lets us actually see what's inside the pipe rather than inferring it from symptoms alone. That visual confirmation is often the difference between confidently recommending the right method the first time and guessing based on how often a drain has clogged.

Grease And Kitchen Habits

A Preventable Contributor Worth Naming

Pouring cooking grease down a kitchen drain, even in small amounts over years, is one of the most common contributors to the buildup that eventually needs hydro jetting to fully clear. Cooling grease in a container and disposing of it in the trash is a small habit that measurably reduces how often this becomes an issue.

A Quick Recap

The Short Version If You're Skimming

Snaking is faster and cheaper for a single, isolated clog, while hydro jetting fully cleans a pipe's interior and is the better call for recurring buildup or root intrusion. A quick diagnosis, sometimes with a camera, tells us honestly which one your drain actually needs.

FAQs

Common Questions

Can I request hydro jetting even for a simple clog?

You can, but if a simple snake would resolve it, we'll tell you honestly rather than upsell a more expensive method you don't need.

Does hydro jetting work on cast iron pipe?

Generally yes, though a camera inspection first confirms the pipe's condition can handle the pressure, especially on older, more corroded lines.

Why did my clog come back a few weeks after snaking?

Snaking clears a path through a blockage but doesn't remove buildup along the rest of the pipe wall, which is exactly the kind of recurring pattern hydro jetting is built to address.

How long does hydro jetting usually take?

A typical residential hydro jetting job takes one to two hours, longer than a comparable snaking job, since it treats the pipe's full length rather than a single point.

Can hydro jetting be used as regular preventive maintenance?

For a home with a documented history of grease buildup, an occasional preventive jetting can make sense, though it isn't necessary for most healthy lines.

Is hydro jetting noisy or disruptive to the rest of the house?

It's no more disruptive than typical plumbing work at the access point, and the rest of the house is unaffected during the process.

Can hydro jetting damage a very old, fragile pipe if not done carefully?

It can if the pipe is already severely compromised, which is exactly why we recommend a camera check first on older lines before jetting.

Will hydro jetting clear a clog caused by a foreign object stuck in the line?

Not always, a lodged object sometimes needs mechanical retrieval rather than water pressure alone, which a camera inspection would reveal.

Is hydro jetting safe for older homes with delicate plumbing overall?

Yes, when the line's condition has been confirmed first via camera, which is exactly the precaution we take before recommending it on an older system.

Do you offer any kind of guarantee on hydro jetting results?

We stand behind the work we do, and if the same issue returns unexpectedly soon after, we'll take a second look.
