Hurricane Season Plumbing Checklist for Clearwater Homeowners

The City of Clearwater publishes real hurricane-season plumbing guidance every year. Here's what it actually covers, in plain language.

Written by the Clearwater Express Plumbing Team · September 16, 2026

Intro

This Isn't Generic Advice

Every year, the City of Clearwater's Building Department publishes hurricane-season guidance that specifically covers plumbing, securing outdoor fixtures and checking sump pumps before a storm arrives. That's a real, recurring local requirement, not a generic seasonal blog topic, and it's worth taking seriously before storm season is already underway. Most of what follows takes an afternoon and costs little to nothing, which makes it one of the better returns on time you'll get all year.

Step One

Test Your Sump Pump Before You Need It

If your home has a sump pump, run it manually before hurricane season starts. Pour a bucket of water into the pit and confirm it activates and drains properly. A pump that fails during a storm, when you can't easily get a technician out, is a preventable problem.

Step Two

Secure Outdoor Plumbing Fixtures

Outdoor spigots, hose bibs, and any exposed piping should be checked for loose fittings before storm season. Wind-driven debris and flying branches can snap an exposed pipe that would otherwise hold up fine under normal conditions.

Step Three

Know Your Main Shutoff Location

Every homeowner should know exactly where their main water shutoff valve is before an emergency, not during one. If a pipe bursts during or after a storm, being able to shut off water immediately can be the difference between a minor repair and serious water damage.

Step Four

Check Your Water Heater's Strapping

Florida code requires water heaters to be strapped to resist movement during high winds or minor seismic activity. If you're not sure your unit is properly secured, it's worth a quick check before storm season, not after wind has already been an issue.

After The Storm

What To Check Once It Passes

After a storm passes, check for any new dampness around fixtures, unusual water pressure changes, or discoloration in tap water, all of which can indicate storm-related plumbing damage that isn't obvious at first glance.

Irrigation Systems

Don't Forget Backflow Preventers

If your home has an irrigation system, its backflow preventer is another point worth checking before storm season. These devices sit exposed above ground specifically so they can be inspected, but that also makes them vulnerable to wind-driven debris, and a damaged one can affect both your sprinkler system and, in some cases, your home's water supply.

Generator Considerations

If You're Running A Generator During Outages

Some homeowners run well pumps or sump pumps off a generator during extended power outages after a storm. If that applies to you, confirm the generator setup actually works with your specific pump before the emergency arrives, not during it, since compatibility issues are much easier to solve with time to spare.

Stocking Basic Supplies

What's Worth Having On Hand

A few basic supplies, pipe repair tape, a spare wax ring, and a way to shut off water at individual fixtures, are worth keeping on hand before storm season rather than searching for them during an active emergency. None of it is expensive, and having it ready removes one more variable during an already stressful situation.

Renters And Storm Prep

What If You Don't Own The Home

Renters can still test a sump pump if one exists and report a non-functioning unit to a landlord well before storm season rather than during an active warning. Documenting the report in writing protects you if water damage occurs afterward and the pump turns out to have already been reported as faulty.

A Quick Recap

The Short Version If You're Skimming

Test your sump pump, secure outdoor fixtures, know your main shutoff location, and confirm your water heater is properly strapped, all before storm season actually arrives. None of it takes long, and all of it is far easier to do calmly in advance than during an active warning.

Setting A Reminder

Making This An Annual Habit

Since hurricane season arrives on roughly the same calendar window every year, setting a recurring reminder for early June, before the season typically ramps up, turns this checklist into a habit rather than something you remember only after a storm is already approaching.

Working With Neighbors

A Small Community Habit Worth Building

In established neighborhoods, checking in with an elderly or traveling neighbor about their sump pump and outdoor fixtures before a storm is a small gesture that can prevent a genuinely bad outcome, since not everyone is physically able to do these checks themselves every year.

FAQs

Common Questions

Do I really need to test my sump pump every year?

Yes, a sump pump that sat unused for a year can fail silently, and hurricane season is exactly when you'll need it most.

What if I find storm damage to my plumbing after a hurricane?

Shut off water at the main if there's an active leak, and call us. If flooring or drywall has already been damaged, our water damage restoration line handles that specifically.

Should I do this checklist every single year, even after a quiet season?

Yes, a quiet season doesn't mean your fixtures and pump held up fine, it just means they weren't tested. Checking every year regardless of the previous season's activity is the only reliable approach.

Do I need a professional to do any of this, or is it all DIY?

Most of this checklist is genuinely DIY-friendly. Call a professional if you find an issue during your check, like a sump pump that won't activate, rather than trying to repair it yourself under time pressure.

Is there a best time of year to schedule a professional plumbing check?

Late spring, just before hurricane season officially begins, is a reasonable time so any issues found have time to be fixed before storms typically arrive.

What's the single most important item on this checklist if I only have time for one?

Testing the sump pump, if you have one, since a failed pump during an actual storm is one of the more preventable causes of serious water damage.

Does homeowners insurance typically cover storm-related plumbing damage?

It depends on the specific cause and your policy, flood damage and wind damage are often handled differently, so it's worth reviewing your coverage before storm season.

Is it worth having a plumber's number saved before storm season, even without an active issue?

Yes, having a trusted number ready means one less thing to figure out during an actual emergency.
